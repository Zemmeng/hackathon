Problem statement

  • Councils and contractors struggle to foresee how a road closure — alone or overlapping other works — hits traffic, trams and pedestrians.
  • Sign effects are guessed: in London, real diversions were one-fifth of what drivers said they would do (Chatterjee 2002).

Solution overview

  1. Close a lane on the real Melbourne CBD network; add barriers, signs and the exact VMS wording.
  2. Four driver types — commuter, local, tourist, delivery — read each sign (keyword rules today, AI LLM next).
  3. A deterministic engine computes detours, queues and delay, then re-tests each suggested fix.
AI interprets — the engine calculates.

Who uses it & pays

  • Contractors & councils: impact evidence for each permit.
  • RPM Hire: the value of each extra VMS or barrier → the hire quote (planned).
Live demo, 29 Sep: Lonsdale St lane closure, 8 am — engine on real CBD traffic counts

What's different

  • RPM's VMS Preview shows how a sign looks; we show its effect.
  • Mooven forecasts from past delays; we test the wording before deployment.
  • one.network flags clashes; we count the minutes they add.
* Single run (1 lane closed, 8 am); keyword-rule sign reading; assumed traveller mix.
